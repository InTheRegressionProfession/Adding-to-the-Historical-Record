This thesis presents a comparative analysis of different language models available as of mid-2026 on the tasks of optical character recognition (OCR) and named entity recognition (NER) within the historical domain. This subject domain presents a variety of unique challenges that can compound the difficulty of conducting both tasks, one after the other, including but not limited to: data noise, language change and variability, and textual ambiguity, on top of the lack of committed resources inherent to niche disciplines. Using a selection of late 19th century and early 20th century French newspapers, this research project assessed the performance of various models with the additional goal of determining the potential efficacy of low-budget photography as a supplement to expensive archival scanning to expand the availability of historical document digitization. 

As a result, the research performed here identified several emergent trends among the language models tested, namely an evident paradigm shift in historical NER effectiveness away from fine-tuned historical BERT models towards generalist LLM solutions. This is also accompanied by findings that demonstrate that modern LLMs, when implemented to perform the challenging task of OCR and NER within the historical domain, appear capable of effectively handling downstream data noise and maintaining satisfactory performance. Though named entity linking (NEL) appears to be a potential weakpoint for any prospective overall pipeline, the data seems to support the viability of correcting for the inadequacies of low-budget photography with the computational power of modern language models, as well as a need for renewed investigation into the implementation of LLMs within the historical domain, especially for NER analysis.
